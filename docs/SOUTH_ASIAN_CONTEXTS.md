# Optional South Asian and subcontinent contexts — v0.7.0

Sixteen self-selected situations add explanation, four practical actions, an adaptable script and a reflection question to the existing thirteen-domain in-app guidance. All content is original, pre-written educational text. The app does not analyse a narrative, identify a condition or deliver professional mediation.

## Coverage

- Joint-family living, privacy and household work.
- In-law expectations and couple boundaries.
- Marriage pressure, consent or dowry demands — joint mediation disabled.
- “Log kya kahenge”, gossip and reputation pressure.
- Exams, comparison and career expectations.
- Remittances, breadwinner guilt and family requests.
- Migration, diaspora belonging and distance from home.
- Colourism, body comments and marriage comparisons.
- Elder care, sibling roles and gendered care expectations.
- Financial control, restricted movement or family threats — joint mediation disabled.
- Workplace hierarchy, language bias and discrimination.
- Heat, crowded housing, noise and power cuts.
- Floods, displacement and disrupted community life — joint mediation disabled.
- Faith differences, family customs and personal choice.
- Fertility questions, son preference and parenting pressure.
- Rural isolation, transport limits and lack of privacy.

No ethnicity, religion, caste, citizenship, gender or family structure is inferred. The universal response remains the default. These situations can occur in any community. The selector and detailed additions are explicitly labelled English; no independent translation or cultural review is claimed. Faith comfort remains an explicit opt-in.

## Interaction and safety

Choose any support domain, optionally choose a context and submit. The domain explanation remains present; the context supplies the four next steps, a second script and the reflection question. Reset clears the context and generated report. The selector is a native labelled select with explanatory text. Results use the existing focus/announcement flow.

Immediate danger and urgent medical flags override contextual advice. Coercion and minors continue to prevent joint discussion. Forced marriage/dowry demands, financial control/threats and disaster contexts additionally disable that discussion without requiring further disclosure. Advice avoids demanding confrontation, disclosure, evidence collection or leaving without a safe plan. No legal conclusion, visa advice, medical treatment or guaranteed outcome is offered.

Choices stay in memory: no network requests, journal persistence, exports or storage keys are added. The new module is part of the release-scoped atomic offline shell. No dependency, CSP exception or external service is introduced. Publisher resources remain optional.

## Source basis and limits

These are practical examples, not a measurement of how South Asian people behave. They are not a validated assessment or a substitute for a clinician, legal adviser or accredited mediator.

Relevant public background resources checked for this iteration:

- WHO, Stress: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/stress
- WHO, Heat and health: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health
- NELFT NHS, South Asian women focus engagement sessions: https://www.nelft.nhs.uk/news-events/pcref-south-asian-women-focus-engagement-sessions-12908

These sources inform general framing; they do not endorse or validate the specific generated responses. No copyrighted book text is reproduced. Independent clinical, native-language and community review remains pending.

## Verification

New automated checks cover all 16 contexts across 13 domains and three response modes, invalid context fallback, urgent overrides, opt-in faith, safeguarding, module offline inclusion, rendered selection/reset and rendered mediation suppression. Actual CI evidence is recorded in VERIFICATION.md after execution. Real-device, assistive-technology and independent review are not replaced by these tests.
